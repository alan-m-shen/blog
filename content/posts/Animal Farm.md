Every revolution begins with a beautiful dream. On Manor Farm, Old Major gives that dream a voice: overthrow the humans, end exploitation, and build a life in which all animals are equal. The animals succeed in driving out their master. What they fail to secure is a way to hold their new leaders to the promise.

George Orwell's *Animal Farm* follows that gap between victory and freedom. By the final chapter, the promise of equality has become “All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.”[^equality] The disturbing question is how the pigs can turn equality into a defence of privilege while continuing to speak in the revolution's name.

## The First Rule of Tyranny: Control the Past

Napoleon's rule depends on controlling the comparison between the animals' hopes and their actual lives. Squealer supplies statistics that describe abundance while the animals experience hunger. Unable to verify the figures or clearly remember life under Jones, they are pressed to accept the official account over their own experience.[^statistics]

The rewriting of Snowball's history is more aggressive. An early leader of the rebellion becomes a saboteur, then an enemy supposedly allied with Jones from the beginning. His past actions no longer constrain the story told about him. Meanwhile, the commandments on the barn wall acquire qualifications that excuse what the pigs have already done.[^memory]

These changes do more than conceal hypocrisy. They weaken the standard against which the animals might judge their rulers. If every broken promise can be explained as a faulty memory, the revolution cannot be shown to have betrayed its principles: its principles can always be rewritten after the betrayal.

## The Second Rule: An Ignorant Populace Is a Pliant One

The pigs' advantage rests partly on an unequal command of reading and argument. But Orwell's story is more revealing than a simple ban on education. Snowball does organize literacy classes. Some animals learn, some struggle, and Benjamin can read as well as the pigs but usually declines to use that ability.[^education] Knowing how to read and being willing or able to challenge authority are different things.

Under Napoleon, the sheep's slogans interrupt debate and the dogs make disagreement dangerous. After Snowball's expulsion, the Sunday discussions are abolished.[^debate] The animals lose both the opportunity to test an argument and the security needed to oppose it. Squealer's explanations work within that atmosphere of intimidation.

This is why the animals' predicament cannot be reduced to stupidity. Literacy matters, but so do independent discussion and the ability to act together. A commandment painted on a wall offers little protection when the rulers can alter it and punish anyone who objects. The pigs exploit ignorance, then use power to make informed opposition harder.

## The Final Betrayal: Becoming the Enemy

Old Major had named the injustices plainly: humans take the cows' milk, sell the hens' eggs, and will dispose of Boxer when his strength fails. Under the pigs, these forms of exploitation return. The milk is reserved for the pigs, and the hens face starvation when they resist the sale of their eggs.[^exploitation] The new rulers adopt the practices that once justified rebellion against the old ones.

Boxer's fate makes the betrayal personal. He gives the farm his labour and trust until he collapses. The van that carries him away bears a horse slaughterer's name; Squealer insists that it was bought by a veterinary surgeon who had not changed the lettering. Soon afterwards, the pigs acquire money for another case of whisky.[^boxer] The sequence strongly suggests the bargain, while leaving the animals with an official reassurance they desperately want to believe.

The revolution has changed who benefits from Boxer's work without giving him any greater protection. His loyalty, celebrated while he is useful, cannot enforce the promise of a peaceful retirement.

## A Warning for All Time

Orwell's immediate target was Stalinism. In his preface to the Ukrainian edition, he explained that exposing the Soviet myth was necessary to a revival of socialism.[^preface] That context matters: the novel's attack on a regime claiming equality should not be confused with the claim that equality itself is a fraudulent ideal.

Its wider warning concerns the means by which ideals are defended. Democratic supervision and the rule of law require more than reassuring language. People must be able to question an account of events, organize against their leaders, and appeal to rules those leaders cannot rewrite for their own convenience. The farm steadily loses each of those possibilities.

In the final scene, the animals look from pig to man and back again, unable to distinguish them.[^ending] The force of the ending comes from everything that led to it. The pigs have become the enemy through privileges accepted one at a time, objections silenced, and promises revised. A change of rulers can leave the structure of domination intact.
